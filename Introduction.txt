TIME SERIES (Complete Data Science Notes)

====================================================
1. What is Time Series?
====================================================

A Time Series is a sequence of observations collected over time at regular intervals.

Unlike normal datasets where rows are independent, time series data has chronological order, and the order of observations is extremely important.

Examples:
- Stock prices
- Weather data
- Daily sales
- Electricity consumption
- Website traffic
- Heart rate monitoring
- COVID-19 cases
- Cryptocurrency prices

Example:

Date        Sales
01-Jan      100
02-Jan      110
03-Jan      120
04-Jan      115

The value today is often related to previous values.

----------------------------------------------------
2. Why Time Series is Different from Machine Learning?
----------------------------------------------------

Normal Machine Learning:
Rows are independent.
Shuffling rows does not affect the model.

Example:

Age    Salary
22     25000
35     60000
28     40000

Even if rows are shuffled, nothing changes.

Time Series:

Date    Sales
Jan     100
Feb     120
Mar     140

If shuffled:

Mar     140
Jan     100
Feb     120

The information becomes meaningless because time order is lost.

Therefore, time is an important feature.

====================================================
3. Components of Time Series
====================================================

A time series consists of four major components.

Time Series = Trend + Seasonality + Cyclic + Noise

or

Y = T + S + C + R

----------------------------------------------------
A. Trend
----------------------------------------------------

Trend is the long-term increase or decrease in data.

Example:

Year    Sales
2018    200
2019    240
2020    280
2021    340
2022    410

Sales continuously increase.

Types:
- Upward Trend
- Downward Trend
- No Trend

Examples:
- Population growth
- Inflation
- Company revenue

----------------------------------------------------
B. Seasonality
----------------------------------------------------

Seasonality is a repeating pattern occurring at fixed intervals.

Examples:
- Ice cream sales increase every summer.
- Umbrella sales increase every rainy season.
- Shopping increases during Diwali or Christmas.
- Electricity consumption increases every evening.

Characteristics:
- Fixed interval
- Predictable
- Repeats regularly

----------------------------------------------------
C. Cyclic Pattern
----------------------------------------------------

Cycles are long-term fluctuations that do NOT occur at fixed intervals.

Examples:
- Economic growth
- Recession
- Recovery
- Business cycles

Unlike seasonality, cycles may occur after 3 years, 5 years, or 8 years.

----------------------------------------------------
D. Random Noise
----------------------------------------------------

Noise consists of unpredictable fluctuations.

Examples:
- War
- Earthquake
- COVID
- Machine failure
- Stock market crash

Noise cannot be predicted accurately.

6. Stationary Time Series
====================================================

Stationarity is the most important concept in time series.

A stationary series has:

1. Constant Mean
2. Constant Variance
3. Constant Covariance

Example:

3
4
5
4
3
5
4

Mean and variance remain approximately constant.

This is stationary.

----------------------------------------------------
Non-Stationary Example
----------------------------------------------------

20
25
30
35
40
45
50

Mean continuously changes.

This is non-stationary.

====================================================
7. Why Stationarity is Important?
====================================================

Most statistical forecasting algorithms assume stationarity.

Examples:
- AR
- MA
- ARMA
- ARIMA

If data is non-stationary, predictions become unreliable.

====================================================
8. How to Make Data Stationary
====================================================

A. Differencing

Subtract consecutive observations.

Original:

100
120
140
160

Differenced:

20
20
20

Formula:

Yt - Y(t-1)

----------------------------------------------------

B. Log Transformation

Useful when variance increases over time.

Original:

100
300
900
2700

Log:

2.00
2.48
2.95
3.43

Large variations become smaller.

----------------------------------------------------

C. Moving Average

Smooths short-term fluctuations.

Example:

100
110
90
120
100

3-point Moving Average

(100+110+90)/3

====================================================
10. Lag
====================================================

Lag means previous observations.

Example:

Day    Sales

1      100
2      110
3      120
4      130

Lag-1

Today's value depends on yesterday.

Lag-2

Today's value depends on last two days.

====================================================
11. Rolling Window
====================================================

Uses previous k observations.

Example:

3-day Rolling Average

Day4

Average(Day1, Day2, Day3)

Useful for smoothing data.

====================================================
12. Autocorrelation (ACF)
====================================================

Autocorrelation measures correlation between a variable and its previous values.

Question:

Is today's sales related to yesterday's sales?

If yes,
there is autocorrelation.

Used to identify MA terms.

====================================================
13. Partial Autocorrelation (PACF)
====================================================

Measures direct correlation after removing intermediate lag effects.

Used to identify AR terms.

====================================================
14. Difference Between ACF and PACF
====================================================

ACF
- Total correlation
- Includes indirect effects
- Used for MA

PACF
- Direct correlation
- Removes indirect effects
- Used for AR

16. Train-Test Split
====================================================

Never shuffle time series.

Wrong:

shuffle=True

Correct:

Past -------------> Future

Training           Testing

Example:

2019-2023 → Train

2024 → Test

====================================================
17. Evaluation Metrics
====================================================

MAE

Average absolute error.

MAE = Σ|y-ŷ| / n

----------------------------------------------------

MSE

Average squared error.

MSE = Σ(y-ŷ)² / n

----------------------------------------------------

RMSE

Square root of MSE.

Same unit as original data.

----------------------------------------------------

MAPE

Percentage error.

MAPE = (100/n) × Σ| (y-ŷ)/y |

Easy to interpret but problematic when actual values are zero.

====================================================
18. Classical Time Series Models
====================================================

A. Moving Average (MA)

Uses previous forecast errors.

----------------------------------------------------

B. Auto Regression (AR)

Uses previous observations.

----------------------------------------------------

C. ARMA

Combination of AR and MA.

Works only for stationary data.

----------------------------------------------------

D. ARIMA

AR + Integrated + MA

Integrated means differencing.

Handles non-stationary data.

----------------------------------------------------

E. SARIMA

Seasonal ARIMA.

Handles seasonality.

----------------------------------------------------

F. SARIMAX

SARIMA + External Variables.

Example:

Predict Ice Cream Sales using:
- Past Sales
- Temperature
- Holidays

----------------------------------------------------

G. VAR (Vector Auto Regression)

Used for multivariate time series.

Summary
====================================================

Time Series is data collected over time where the order of observations matters. It consists of Trend, Seasonality, Cyclic patterns, and Noise. Before modeling, we analyze stationarity, perform preprocessing (such as differencing or transformations if needed), engineer time-based features, split the data chronologically, train an appropriate forecasting model, evaluate it using metrics like MAE or RMSE, and then use the model to predict future values.
